The Document Expiry Tracking Checklist

Print this. Tick it off. Never get surprised by an expired document again. Every item below has an expiry date — the question is whether you know what it is.

How to use this checklist: Go through each category, note the expiry date for anything you own or hold, and add it to a tracker (or a calendar — but ideally a tracker, so you get reminders automatically). Documents you don't track are the ones that expire on you.

👤 Personal Documents

The documents tied to your identity and ability to travel. These are the ones with the longest renewal times — and the harshest consequences for missing.

  • Passport

    Renew 9–12 months before expiry. Most countries require 6 months of validity beyond your return date. Full guide →

  • Visas (tourist, work, student, residence)

    Each has its own expiry and renewal process. Track them separately from your passport.

  • Driver's license

    Check whether your jurisdiction allows online renewal or requires an in-person visit — in-person can mean weeks of lead time.

  • International driving permit (IDP)

    Valid for 1 year from issue. Easy to forget if you only travel occasionally.

  • National ID card

    Required for domestic travel, banking, and government services in most countries.

  • Vehicle registration & inspection

    Registration renews annually or biannually; inspections are often required before registration can be renewed.

  • Professional certifications

    Medical, legal, contractor, teaching, real estate — most require continuing education credits before renewal.

  • Trusted traveller programmes (Global Entry, TSA PreCheck, NEXUS)

    Memberships expire every 5 years. Renewal can take months to process.

🏢 Business & Compliance Documents

If you run a business, these are the ones with the highest penalty for a missed renewal — fines, forced closures, or lost contracts. See our business license tracking page for a deeper look.

  • Business operating license

    Issued by your local or national authority. Renewal windows are often strict. Full guide →

  • Sales tax / VAT registration

    Re-registration is required in some jurisdictions, even if the number itself doesn't change.

  • Health & safety inspections

    Restaurants, salons, gyms, and any physical location — inspection certificates usually expire annually.

  • Professional / industry certifications

    Required to legally operate in some industries — legal, medical, financial, construction.

  • Building permits & occupancy certificates

    Often overlooked after the initial build. Some require periodic renewal.

  • Import / export licenses

    Customs and trade authorisations, especially for regulated goods.

  • Environmental or waste permits

    Required for many industrial and agricultural operations.

  • Fire safety certificates

    Usually annual. Your landlord may hold it, but you're still required to comply.

💰 Financial, Insurance & Legal

A lapsed policy or a missed renewal window here can cost you a claim, a contract, or serious money. See our insurance tracking page for the full picture on coverage gaps.

  • General liability insurance

    Required by most B2B contracts and commercial leases. Renewal notices often go to old addresses.

  • Professional liability / E&O

    Required for consultants, agencies, and most service providers.

  • Workers' compensation

    Legally required in most jurisdictions if you have employees. Non-negotiable.

  • Commercial auto & property insurance

    Both renew annually. Premiums change — check the renewal quote before auto-renewing.

  • Cyber liability & D&O insurance

    Increasingly required by enterprise clients, boards, and investors.

  • Vendor certificates of insurance (COIs)

    You need to track their expiry dates too. Full guide →

  • Home / renters insurance

    Personal policies expire annually. Check that contents coverage still matches what you own.

  • Contract renewals & cancellation windows

    Track the cancellation deadline, not the end date. Full guide →

  • Commercial lease & property agreements

    Renewal windows are typically 90–180 days before the end date.

  • Credit card expirations

    Easy to miss, and a failure point for auto-payments (see Digital below).

💻 Digital Assets & Infrastructure

These are the silent killers. Nothing makes your website, email, or app stop working faster than an expired certificate or domain — and they always expire on a Friday.

  • Domain names

    Set to auto-renew, but also track the expiry — auto-renew fails silently if the card on file expired.

  • SSL / TLS certificates

    Now often auto-renewed (Let's Encrypt, etc.) but worth tracking. Manual certs expire on a 1-year cycle.

  • Software licenses

    Desktop software, design tools, and industry-specific programs often use annual licenses.

  • SaaS subscriptions

    Every tool has a renewal date. Track the ones with annual terms — those are the ones where you have leverage to negotiate.

  • API keys & access tokens

    Many have expiration policies (30, 90, or 365 days). Track them if they don't auto-rotate.

  • Cloud hosting & CDN contracts

    Annual reserved instances and committed-use discounts renew on a fixed date.

  • Trademarks & patents

    Renewal cycles vary by jurisdiction (often 10 years for trademarks), but missing a renewal can mean losing the mark entirely.

  • Business bank account verifications

    Some jurisdictions require periodic re-verification. Missing it can freeze the account.

✅ How to set this up in 10 minutes

A checklist is only useful if the dates end up somewhere that will remind you. Here's the process:

  1. Gather the documents. Physically or digitally — pull out everything on the list above. A folder, a filing cabinet, a password manager, wherever they live.
  2. Note every expiry date. Write them on a piece of paper as you go. Don't try to organise them yet — just get the dates.
  3. Add them to a tracker. A calendar works, but only if you set multiple reminders per item — which is tedious. A dedicated tracker does this automatically and shows everything on one dashboard.
  4. Set your reminder rules. For anything with a renewal process (passport, license), 90 days is a good minimum. For anything that just needs a payment, 30 days is fine. For anything critical (insurance, business license), 90 days minimum.
  5. Review quarterly. Once every 3 months, open the dashboard and check what's expiring in the next 90 days. This is where you catch problems before they become emergencies.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I review my expiry dates?

Quarterly. Anything more frequent is overkill for most people; anything less and you'll miss renewal windows that open 60–90 days before expiry.

What's the single most important document to track?

For individuals: your passport. For businesses: your business license and general liability insurance. Both have long renewal lead times and severe consequences for missing them.

Can I just use a spreadsheet?

You can, but spreadsheets are passive — they don't remind you. They also live on one device, which fails as soon as more than one person is involved. A dedicated tracker is worth it once you're tracking more than a handful of items.

How far in advance should I set reminders?

90 days for anything with a renewal process (passports, licenses, insurance, certifications). 30 days for anything that's just a payment. 7 days as a final failsafe for everything.
