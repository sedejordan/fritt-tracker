Nigerian Business Compliance Checklist: Documents and Filings to Renew Every Year

By the Fritt Tracker team

Running a business in Nigeria means answering to several authorities at once. The Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC), tax authorities, pension and insurance bodies, sector regulators, state governments and local councils each issue their own documents, and each has its own renewal date. Miss one and the cost can be a penalty, a delayed contract or a business that is no longer in good standing.

This checklist covers the documents and filings most Nigerian businesses need to keep current. It is a starting point, not legal advice. Requirements differ by sector, state and company type, so confirm details with the relevant authority.

CAC annual returns

Under the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020, registered companies must file annual returns with the CAC every year, whether or not they traded. The CAC has said that penalties can apply to the company and to each of its directors or officers, and it has moved to remove inactive or noncompliant companies from the register.

A public notice dated 15 July 2026, as reported in the press, listed around 100,000 companies for striking off and gave them 90 days to file outstanding annual returns, including persons with significant control and beneficial ownership information. If you have not checked your own company's status recently, do it now on the CAC website at cac.gov.ng.

Tax registration and clearance

Keep your tax identification details in order and file your returns on time. Many government contracts, tenders, bank facilities and licence applications ask for a tax clearance certificate, so note its validity period and renew it before you need to submit it.

Employee related certificates

If you have staff, look at the pension, employee compensation and training fund requirements that apply to your business, and at the compliance certificates attached to them. Buyers and procurement teams often ask for these as proof of good standing. Group life insurance for employees is another item to track where the law requires it.

Sector licences and product registrations

Regulated businesses carry extra documents. Food, drug and cosmetic products need registration with NAFDAC, which is valid for a fixed number of years before it must be renewed. Manufacturers and importers may hold standards certifications from SON. Other sectors such as transport, energy, health and finance have their own regulators, licences and renewal cycles.

Vehicles and drivers

If your business runs vehicles, each one carries several dates: vehicle licence, road worthiness, insurance and any permits your state or route requires. Each driver adds a driver's licence expiry. A fleet of ten vehicles can quickly mean dozens of dates to watch.

Premises, insurance and contracts

Track your lease or tenancy end date, business premises permits from your state or local government, signage or advertising permits, and every insurance policy. Add contract end dates and renewal windows too, because a lapsed agreement with a key client or supplier is just as damaging as a lapsed licence.

Staff and foreign worker documents

Professional bodies typically require regular renewal of practising licences, and foreign staff need valid residence and work documents. Keep these on the list under each person's name.

How to build a renewal calendar that works

  1. List every document. Start with everything above, then ask each department what it holds.
  2. Record the expiry date, the issuing body and the person responsible for renewal.
  3. Decide how early you need warning. If a renewal takes three weeks to process, a reminder one week before expiry is too late. Many businesses use 90, 60 and 30 days.
  4. Send the reminder to a named person, not only a shared inbox.
  5. After each renewal, update the new expiry date straight away and note the cost.

Frequently asked questions

How often do companies file annual returns in Nigeria?

Every year. The Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 requires registered companies to file annual returns with the CAC each year, even if the company is inactive.

What happens if a Nigerian company does not file annual returns?

The CAC can apply penalties to the company and to its directors or officers, and it can strike a company off the register. It has publicly announced striking off exercises for companies that fall behind. Check the CAC website for your company's current status.

What documents should a small business in Nigeria track?

At minimum, CAC filings, tax documents and clearance certificates, insurance policies, premises and lease dates, vehicle papers, sector licences and staff certifications. Add anything else that has an expiry or renewal date.
